SCENARIO: an overcast afternoon just after rain, a wet riverside promenade in Hangang Park, Seoul. He walks toward the camera with a plain matte-black VR headset over his eyes, playing a Korean-learning racing game. RIGHT hand: exactly ONE generic unbranded clear takeaway cup of iced americano with a lightweight flat plastic lid. LEFT hand: one phone. Black backpack; the plush hangs from its right side on a short chain.
0.0-1.6s SLIP, real time. Low wide angle at pavement level. He leans hard to his right as if cornering in the game; his white sneaker slides on the wet stone and he tips backward off balance in a light comedic slip, arms flying up. The headset stays on over his eyes; below it his mouth opens in surprise.
COFFEE RELEASE: his grip opens and the cup leaves his RIGHT hand with one short outward, slightly upward rotational impulse. It travels only 20 to 35 cm. The cup tilts and rotates. Because of the rotation and the liquid's inertia ONE edge of the lid partially lifts; the opposite side stays attached; a REAL gap forms. ONLY AFTER the gap exists does coffee begin escaping: one restrained dark ribbon and a few connected odd-sized droplets. RIGHT HAND = EMPTY from that instant, fingers spread. The same jolt swings the plush outward on its chain; it stays attached.
