The three rules you'll use every day

Korean has one letter, ㄹ. Where it sits in the word decides the sound.

사랑sa·rang

Before a vowel: a quick tap. The tongue tip flicks the ridge behind your top teeth once — like the tt in American “better”, or the r in Spanish “pero”. It is not the English R.

물mul

End of a syllable: a clear L. Before a consonant or at the end of the word, keep the tongue tip touching behind your top teeth.

빨리ppalli

ㄹ + ㄹ: a double L. Hold one long L across the two syllables.

The official romanization follows the same idea: ㄹ is written r before a vowel, l before a consonant or at the end, and ll for ㄹㄹ — Guri (구리), Imsil (임실), Ulleung (울릉).

…and the surprises

서울에[서우레]

An ending ㄹ slides over. 서울 ends with L, but add a vowel and the ㄹ moves to the next syllable — now it's a tap.

신라Silla

ㄴ next to ㄹ becomes ㄹ. 신라 sounds like [실라], 설날 like [설랄].

종로Jongno

After ㅁ, ㅇ, ㄱ or ㅂ, ㄹ turns into ㄴ. 종로 sounds like [종노], 국립 like [궁닙].

닭[닥]

Sometimes it's silent. In ㄺ, ㄻ and ㄿ at the end of a word, the ㄹ drops: 닭 sounds like [닥], 삶 like [삼].

Where these rules come from

  • National Institute of Korean Language, Standard Pronunciation (표준 발음법) — Art. 10–13, 19, 20, 29.
  • Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, Revised Romanization of Korean (국어의 로마자 표기법), Notice 2014-42 — Ch. 2 Art. 2 Note 2, Ch. 3 Art. 1.
